## The influence meter, re-briefed (answers the note's Q1 directly)

The meter pack was rebuilt in the game's UI Toolkit terms, aligned to the owner's D5, and re-sent to Chevon as `influence-meter.zip` (prompt: "implement this"). It is not uploaded to either repository by this session. Meanwhile the game session had already drawn the meter from the first pack's layers (`origin/main` 0b2f4b5: `MatchScreen.Influence.cs` `BuildMeter`, `SetMeter`, `MeterNumber`; layers at `Resources/art/meter/`; the first pack at `docs/ui-kit-cut/influence-meter/`), so the pack is now the record that build is measured against, plus what the first pack lacked. What it settles:

- **The fill rule is D5.** The bar is always full: gold from your well, blue from theirs, meeting at the point that matches the two totals' proportion, the diamond on the meeting point; a side with nothing has no fill at all; level, and nothing on either side, is half and half. The pack's earlier gap rule (and an interim "pilot light" stub this session had drafted) is withdrawn; the composites, manifest and reference element now follow D5 and match the game's `SetMeter`.
- **UI Toolkit only.** `IMPLEMENT.md` and `unity/InfluenceMeterElement.cs` speak `UIDocument`, `PanelSettings`, `VisualElement`, `Label` and the game's own `Art.Tex`, `Art.Abs`, `Art.Slice`, `Art.Inter` helpers. No Canvas Scaler, RectTransform, uGUI Image or TextMeshPro instruction remains. The art ships as the cut pipeline expects: separate PNG layers, a manifest with positions, nine-slice borders and the two number seats.
- **Number wells are live.** Each well carries a `Label` centred on its navy disc (measured 216 px across on the 2400 px canvas, 9.0% of the meter's width; the game fills the well element with the Label and centres it, which lands on the same centre). One size for one- and two-digit values.
- **14 pt, measured.** Inter SemiBold 4.001 (the static Google Fonts cut, read with fontTools): tabular figures are 1325/2048 em, so at 14 pt every digit is 9.06 pt, "0", "1" and "8" are 9.1 pt wide and "11", "88" and "99" are 18.1 pt, matching the game session's F3. Inter's default figures are proportional (a 1 is 866/2048 em), so tabular must be baked (UI Toolkit cannot toggle OpenType features); without baking, "11" is 6.1 pt narrower than "88". The game's `Resources/fonts/` on `origin/main` carries the `-Tnum` cuts and `Art.Inter(..., tabular: true)` picks them; the game sets the size as 0.54 × the well's width, 14 pt at its 26 pt wells.
- **The disc versus the width.** At the game's placement (216 pt for the wells' outer edges) the disc is about 21 pt and "88" leaves 1.4 pt of air each side. A quarter-em of air each side would need a 279 pt meter, wider than a Location column. That is the tradeoff behind 14 pt, stated for Chevon to accept or change; the creative director has recommended 14.
- **Three digits.** The engine has no numeric cap (`Engine/Query.cs` `InfluenceAt` only floors at 0). Per side a Location holds 5 Inside and 3 at the Gates (`Engine/Rules.cs` lines 33 and 34); the highest printed Influence in `Resources/content.json` is 8 (Mansa Musa) and Inside pays +1; lasting bonuses add +1 per Character on a rebuild or a left-behind Threat (`Engine/Setup.cs` lines 448 and 532). Eight top Characters would read 69 before lasting bonuses over an 8-turn match (9 extended) from a 24-card deck, so three digits are not reachable in ordinary play, but nothing in the rules forbids them and no match log was searched. Proposed treatment for the director to confirm: keep 14 pt and let a three-digit value (27.2 pt) overrun the disc rather than shrink or abbreviate; neither the game's `MeterNumber` nor the reference element clips or auto-sizes, so that is what happens today. No two-digit cap is added to the rules.
- **Proof values** 0, 1, 8, 11, 88, 99 are named in the pack; the optical centring check and the 0 v 9 pose are Chevon's in Unity.

The geometry stayed as approved: left gold, right blue, the proportional shared track, the meeting-point diamond, the glow pinned at each rim. UI Kit Maker does not redraw it.

HOW THE BROWSER GETS THE FACES. Nothing serif is bundled: Literata, Crimson Pro and Cinzel are loaded at runtime from Google Fonts by ensureFont, which appends one <link> per family to fonts.googleapis.com/css2 using the GAME_FONTS css string, so the live site asks for VARIABLE fonts (Literata ital,opsz,wght 200..900; Crimson Pro ital,wght 200..900; Cinzel wght 400..900). With Google reachable, Chromium registers one variable face per subset (measured: 'Literata | 200 900', 'Cinzel | 400 900') and every weight inside the axis is a true instance, not synthetic. Inter is the one bundled face: @fontsource-variable/inter 5.2.8 (OFL-1.1) registers 'Inter Variable' 100..900 as woff2-variations; the live CSS carries it and the latin woff2 serves (48256 bytes, font/woff2). BUT the registered family name is 'Inter Variable', and the renderer uses two stacks: kit text uses "'<font>', 'Inter Variable', Inter, sans-serif" (21 sites in the live bundle), while the instrument voice (HUD readouts, counters, badges) uses font-family="Inter, sans-serif" (65 sites in the live bundle). That second stack never names the bundled face, so on the live site instrument text wears a locally installed Inter if the viewer has one, otherwise the OS sans-serif (measured here: Liberation Sans Bold [system]). A kit that picks Inter as its font does reach the bundled face via the fallback position.

SANDBOX VERSUS LIVE. In this sandbox the shot-board loader stubs fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com and serves three local static cuts, each declared at weights 400 through 900: Cinzel Bold (a 228-glyph subset, usWeightClass 700), Crimson Pro SemiBold (306-glyph subset, 600) and 'Literata 12pt Bold' (google/fonts repo static, 700). So every sandbox screenshot paints Literata 600/700/800/900 with the one Bold cut, Crimson Pro 500/600/700 with the one SemiBold cut, and Inter with no Inter at all: the vite dev server here serves the bundled Inter woff2 from /@fsui-kit-maker/node_modules/... which returns 403, so the latin 'Inter Variable' face is in status 'error' and both 'Inter' and 'Inter Variable' paint Liberation Serif/Sans. The live site does not have that problem (the latin woff2 serves 200). A no-stub run here partly reached Google Fonts (Literata variable loaded; Cinzel registered 400..900) but most css2 requests failed with net::ERR_TOO_MANY_RETRIES and Crimson Pro never registered, so the stub remains the honest sandbox road and the handbook's 'Google Fonts are blocked' should read 'unreliable'.

### Q05. Which shine controls actually exist?

### Q06. How are wipe masks and rim masks represented?

**Verified finding.** Plain language. (1) Masks today: every shell piece carries two clipPaths, `fc` (the face, the silhouette inset by the bevel wall) and `oc` (the outer silhouette). The face paint, the gloss-above, the specular and the idle wipe band all clip to `fc`. The rim is not a mask at all: it is a stroked inset copy of the silhouette drawn in the shell group. The edge shine is a stroke of the face path with pathLength=100 and no mask. (2) Reflections under editable text: the gloss can and does pass under the words when its layer is 'below' (the default, and what every Nightfall fork uses): it is painted inside the face group before the content group that holds the live <text> and glyph. Everything else passes OVER the words: gloss 'above', the specular, the idle edge spark, and the idle wipe band, which addShine drops in as the last child of the lift group unless a piece emits a `data-shine-seat` placeholder (the mechanism exists; no shell piece emits one today). In Unity the WipeShine behaviour creates its Mask child at enable time and appends it after the label children, so the band also crosses the text there; only type stamps get a glyph-hugging stencil. (3) Clipping a moving highlight to a chamfered rim while the sprite stretches: the face clip is not shipped as geometry. The exporter ships nine-sliced base sprites per state, an un-sliced specular overlay, a nine-sliced face STENCIL (`base-mask.9.png`) for six families but only when the kit has a face pattern on (Nightfall's pattern is 'none', so none ships for it today), and, when idle.edge is on, 48 evenly spaced arc-length stations sampled from the `fc` clip of each un-sliced base sprite. In Unity the wipe is clipped by a Mask that copies the host sprite and its Sliced type, so it follows the stretched chamfered silhouette correctly, but to the OUTER shell (rim included), not the face. The EdgeShine is deliberately skipped on any nine-sliced sprite because its stations are fractions of the authored canvas; primary, secondary, small, chip, tab, input, panel, header, datarow and slot are the sliceable families, and Nightfall's boards hold 49 stretched or strip items, so no exported Nightfall panel, strip row or wide button would carry an edge spark today. (4) Constant speed through corners: in the app, yes, pathLength=100 normalises the dash offset to arc length so the spark moves at constant speed along the chamfers; in Unity, yes on un-sliced sprites (even arc-length stations, linear index lerp), not available on sliced ones; in the game's CSS, no, the glint is a conic gradient turning at constant angular speed, so its linear speed along the border changes, and the wipe is a background-position slide.

### Q08. Can scheduling be controlled centrally?

**Verified finding.** There is NO central UI animation coordinator in the exported runtime. The only statics in the runtime assembly are shared procedural textures and two flags; there is no singleton, budget, scheduler or event bus. CANCEL ON DRAG/PRESS/RESOLVE: not present. ### Q15. Which work needs a separate engine or exporter change?

**Verified finding.** Two of the four capabilities already exist in the engine and the export; two do not. Timed shine motion in the export exists: cfg.idle (wipe, edge, freq, blend, wipeDur, edgeDur, wipeWidth, trigger) renders as CSS keyframes in the app and ships as a manifest block plus two runtime MonoBehaviours, WipeShine and EdgeShine, that the importer attaches to prefabs and re-converges on re-import; both tick on unscaled time. Nightfall has both flags off (inherited from Stand on Business; the look writer never sets idle), so turning it on is a kit change, with a re-export because the edge outline rows are only sampled when the edge flag is on at export time. A rim-traversing highlight exists as EdgeShine: a spark walking 48 even arc-length stations of the face outline at constant speed, but the stations are stored as image fractions and mapped linearly onto the RectTransform, so on a nine-slice-stretched piece the corners drift off the chamfer and the horizontal legs run faster than the vertical ones; the spark colour is a fixed white field the importer never tunes. Central scheduling is missing: every shine keeps its own clock with a name-hash stagger, nothing enforces one pass at a time, nothing cancels on press, drag or resolve, and neither runtime checks interactable (the app suppresses the edge shine on the disabled state; Unity does not). Reduced motion is missing in Unity: the app honours prefers-reduced-motion, no PatternBreak runtime reads any reduce-motion setting. Each gap closes with a contained change in engineExport.ts (the runtime templates, the manifest block and two importer helpers), none of it a rewrite.
